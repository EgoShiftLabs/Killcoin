November 18, 2026 • Strategic and creative operating plan • Working version

The position

A token can be launched in minutes. A world people want to return to cannot. THE AGENCY is being developed as an original noir entertainment property: recurring characters, connected stories, a recognizable visual language, participatory missions, an editorial intelligence desk, and creative commissions. KILL COIN, also known as $HITLIST, is a related crypto project. It is not the admission ticket to the Agency.

We are not selling an assertion that longevity is guaranteed. We are making choices that give longevity a chance: own original IP, produce consistently, preserve audience trust, protect contributor rights, test demand before spending, and keep the universe usable even if the token attracts no lasting market interest.

1. Identity: one universe, distinct operations

THE AGENCY — the independent fictional organization and creative brand. It owns its canon, cast, recurring formats, design language and community experience. Its stories do not require a blockchain or a particular video game.

KILL COIN / $HITLIST — the related crypto identity, community and optional discovery channel. Holding, purchasing, staking or connecting a wallet is not required for Agency participation. Financial relationships, token-linked incentives and creator rewards must be clearly disclosed when relevant.

WATCHTOWER — a moderated, public-information desk for crypto project and narrative nominations. Private intake and editorial review are separate from the public case registry. Nominations do not establish misconduct.

OPERATIONS — original animation, action/comedy shorts, fictional dossiers, cinematic storytelling and, where authorized, optional gameplay-based experiments. The main creative product must be able to stand without Rockstar footage.

BOUNTY DESK — modest, funded creative briefs for posters, scripts, edits, music, voice work and original art. These are commissions, not incentives to harass or target real people.

2. What makes the Agency different

The measure of the project will not be how loudly it launches. It will be what remains after the launch cycle: identifiable characters; episodes worth replaying; fans who recognize a voice or silhouette before seeing a logo; a growing body of original assets; fair, credited collaborations; and a community that can participate without buying a token.

Our advantage is not a claim to be the only long-term builder in crypto. It is a verifiable operating difference: published creative output, a living canon, reviewed rather than automatic Watchtower cases, a working archive, and visible milestones tied to proof of execution. The Agency should earn trust through releases, not declarations.

3. The cast: twelve operatives, three lead introductions

Twelve operatives already form the visual roster. Version 1.1 changes the rollout: develop all twelve in a controlled story bible, but introduce just three as recurring leads during the first season. The other nine remain available for reveals, rivalries, alliances and audience-led creative arcs. We will choose the first three based on story potential, visual distinction and production practicality; this document does not assign invented names or biographies.

Each operative receives: codename; design reference and silhouette; role and motivation; voice and speaking rhythm; personal code; specific weakness; signature behavior or ritual; relationships and rivalries; permitted visual uses; and rights/ownership record. Perfect, invincible characters are less interesting than ones who make costly choices, lose face, argue and sometimes fail.

THE AGENCY itself is a recurring presence: a mysterious organization with rules, an internal chain of command, tensions between departments, and consequences for breaking its code. Its mythology creates continuity between otherwise independent shorts.

4. The production engine

Three repeatable lanes anchor the first 90 days. AGENT FILES: 15–30 second character moments, voice tests, rituals and rivalries. AGENCY OPERATIONS: 15–60 second original fictional missions, comedic reversals and noir episodes. WATCHTOWER FILES: concise, sourced satire about crypto projects and public narratives, published only after review. A longer monthly debrief can connect story threads and highlight creators.

Start at two quality shorts per week when capacity permits; a 12-short minimum over 90 days is the floor, with 20–24 as a stretch target, not a guaranteed output. Use a reusable script pattern, simple shot list, a small library of owned sound cues, 9:16 delivery templates, captions, and batch recording/editing. Measure watch-through, rewatches, saves, shares, qualified replies and character recall, not views alone.

Each format must have a reason to return: a developing rivalry, an unfinished mission, an agent whose judgment is questionable, or a case narrative with a documented update. We are building episodes, not a rotating catalog of anonymous promotional graphics.

5. Participation is a role, not a wallet balance

Community members can select an operative to follow, propose original story prompts, submit art or scripts, join opt-in fictional divisions, and contribute to creative challenges. Participation grants no governance rights or financial claim unless a separate arrangement is explicitly documented. No one needs to buy $HITLIST to join.

Community creations require prior permission before reposting, attribution by the creator's chosen name or alias, and a written license defining whether the Agency can edit, distribute, monetize or use the work in merchandise. Creator commissions are scoped, priced and funded before work begins; winners and payment conditions are announced clearly.

6. WATCHTOWER: intelligence without a mob

The intended sequence is: anonymous form submission → private Google response sheet → moderator screening and evidence review → canonical project/contract grouping → editorial approval → separate public-approved spreadsheet → automated JSON publication → searchable case registry. The public website must never query the raw response sheet.

Eligible subjects are public crypto projects, contracts, public claims and market narratives. Watchtower does not publish private-person targets. Cases require public evidence links, a precise description of what can be observed, and review of context; an anonymous claim alone is insufficient. Visible categories and status labels are editorial and satirical, not court findings.

Ranking means the number of reviewed, eligible nominations associated with a project identifier, not unique voters and not proof of wrongdoing. Anonymous submissions can be duplicated or coordinated, so moderators may reject repeated, fabricated, abusive or off-topic entries. No real-person targeting or automated X-message campaigns are triggered by submissions. Optional public X profiles are for contextual verification and never authorize intimidation.

The theatrical BURN PROTOCOL has a strict order: validate input, save the private submission, receive a successful response, play the visual destruction, then clear local fields. If saving fails, the interface must not pretend the file was received. Provide an accessible skip option and a meaningful error message.

Publish an evidence standard, an understandable corrections/removal request process, a review log and a privacy summary before promoting the public rankings. Add a second trusted reviewer when case volume justifies it. A recurring Watchtower segment should earn credibility through accuracy as well as humor.

7. Games and cinematic worlds: opportunities, not dependencies

Grand Theft Auto VI is scheduled to launch November 19, 2026 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S as a single-player experience at release. There is no confirmed GTA VI custom-roleplay-server platform. The Agency will not announce a GTA VI server, multiplayer launch date, or token-linked game feature.

A low-cost experiment may use original Agency scripts, voiceover, licensed music/SFX and carefully reviewed gameplay content only where the relevant rights permit it. The preferred foundation remains original footage, animation and independent assets. Rockstar's Creator Platform License Agreement restricts commercial association with third-party brands and cryptocurrency promotion in the creator-server context. A distinct name does not remove those restrictions.

FiveM for GTA V is a possible later technical learning path, not an automatic brand activation. A private roleplay pilot would require permitted use, opt-in players, safe fictional contracts, moderators, a documented appeals process and an acceptable legal/commercial structure. No real-world targets or harassment of unconsenting players.

8. Funding, token separation and creator economics

The Agency's first content should not depend on token price appreciation, a token treasury or hypothetical future trading revenue. Any creator rewards or token-related proceeds used to fund productions must be disclosed in the appropriate context and budgeted only after funds actually exist. There is no promise of financial return to Agency members or contributors.

Potential revenue, if demonstrated and permitted, includes original character licensing, standalone merchandise, independent art editions, production commissions, distribution partnerships and other entertainment rights. Each requires rights clearance and evidence of demand. Gameplay monetization and server activities require separate platform-permission review.

Capital rule: the first expensive device must solve a proven production problem. A console is optional for clip testing; a gaming PC is conditional on creator workflow or a serious roleplay pilot; rented hosting comes last. No physical server purchase is planned.

9. Technology and current implementation status

Live foundation: the public GitHub Pages KILL COIN site, a separate Agency registry page, an approved-only JSON schema, a GitHub Actions sync workflow, and private/public Google Sheet workbooks. The Google Form is linked to the private spreadsheet; question cleanup, anonymous end-to-end submission testing, public CSV publishing/configuration, moderator-to-ranking mapping, and successful Burn-after-save wiring remain launch gates as of November 18, 2026.

This is deliberately an equipment-light architecture. The founder can run the editorial MVP with GitHub Pages, Google Forms/Sheets, a review checklist and social media. If volume, abuse or authentication needs exceed the Sheets model, evaluate an API/database migration with data minimization and access controls. No token wallet is needed for submission.

10. Governance and risk discipline

The founder is the creative and editorial owner at MVP. Add a second reviewer and moderator when submission volume or production activity makes single-person oversight unreliable. Maintain a changelog for canon, licenses for commissioned work, records of moderation decisions, and a working public correction/removal channel.

No offline targeting, doxxing, threats, coordinated harassment, claims that an allegation has been legally proven without such a finding, or gameplay harassment of unwilling players. The fictional Agency can be hard-edged and outrageous; real-world editorial decisions must be controlled, documented and reversible.

Critical risks include weak character attachment, inconsistent production, founder exhaustion, crypto-market contamination of the brand, anonymous ballot stuffing, false or defamatory submissions, platform license changes, poor IP chain-of-title and equipment overspend. The response is not a longer disclaimer: it is better production practice, evidence review, ownership records and clear stop/go gates.

11. The first 90 days: November 18, 2026 – February 16, 2027

GATE A | Nov 18–Dec 15, 2026: Finish the private-to-reviewed-to-public Watchtower workflow; test one successful and one failed submission; publish policies and a correction channel; complete story-bible briefs for all twelve; select three leads; establish visual/audio templates. Exit only when private responses cannot leak to the public feed.

GATE B | Dec 16, 2026–Jan 15, 2027: Release six original shorts across at least two formats; introduce lead characters; measure 3-second holds, completions, saves and substantive discussions. If gameplay is used, document rights and clearance first. A console purchase is optional, not a deadline.

GATE C | Jan 16–Feb 16, 2027: Publish at least six additional shorts; trial one moderated community creative brief; establish a repeatable editing cadence; review character recognition and returning contributors. Stretch goal: 20–24 total shorts across the 90 days if production quality remains high.

Review on Feb 17, 2027: Continue, adjust or pause formats based on retention, returning commenters, character recognition, community contributions, Watchtower review quality and time/cash cost. A growing backlog of unreviewed cases or a failing publishing cadence is a reason to slow down, not spend more.

12. Later milestones, strictly conditional

March–June 2027: develop Season One story arcs, pursue targeted creator collaborations and test merchandise concepts only if the audience asks for them. Consider a gaming PC only after content data supports a roleplay or production need. No GTA VI server timetable.

July–September 2027: optional GTA V/FiveM learning or restricted private tests only if the licensing path is acceptable, 10–20 willing participants exist and two moderators are available. Rent a hosted test environment monthly, rather than buying physical server hardware.

October–December 2027: choose among scaling original animation/IP, expanding a permitted roleplay experiment, seeking a licensing partnership, or dropping multiplayer and retaining the successful content formats. Successful independence from any particular game counts as progress.

13. What success can actually be measured by

Creative: three lead operatives independently recognizable to returning viewers; a controlled twelve-character canon; at least twelve finished shorts in the 90-day pilot; positive repeat engagement rather than a single viral spike.

Community: original contributions with documented rights; useful editorial nominations rather than brigading; at least 25 substantive interactions or creations as an initial learning target; manageable moderation response times.

Systems and capital: no raw private data published; every public case reviewed and sourced; visible correction path; no token-based access requirement; no server bought to chase a future hypothetical; spending linked to demonstrated use.

14. What this paper does not promise

No investment return; no guarantee that a meme coin survives; no guaranteed longevity or fandom; no assertion of misconduct based only on a nomination; no unique-voter claim; no guaranteed creator payout unless a funded brief is accepted; no affiliation with Rockstar; and no promised GTA VI multiplayer, custom-server access or commercial usage license.

Our position is simpler: build something worth watching, participating in and preserving. Then let the evidence determine what to expand.
